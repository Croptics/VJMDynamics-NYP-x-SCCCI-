# AI Reflection — Jun Qi (InsightMetrics)

I built the base platform the rest of the team's features sit on: the admin
dashboard, login and sessions, accounts and permissions, delegate management,
the activity history, multi-checkpoint attendance, escalations, announcements,
export and the offline write queue. My AI logs are in `ai/jq/ai-logs/`.

## Where AI helped

Looking back through my logs, the sessions fall into three kinds of work.

The first is reviewing other people's changes before I integrated them. When
Vimal pushed to `INTv2` with commit messages like "ee" and "er", I used Claude
to go through the actual diff and tell me what had really changed, rather than
guessing from the message. That is how I knew his commits included a fresh
database fix and a fix for an endpoint that was returning delegate email
addresses to anyone who asked.

The second is debugging my own code. The clearest example is
`resetArrivedBeforeNextCheckpoint()` in
`backend/routes/dashboard/checkpoints.js`, where an Arrived delegate was not
being reset before the next stop and I needed to work out why.

The third is deployment and database decisions. When the team was looking at
moving to Supabase, working through it with Claude is what made it clear that it
was not a connection-string change for us — it would have meant porting the data
layer, which is not what people assumed.

## Where I made my own call

**The trip-scope bug.** Mobile was showing a stale trip while desktop showed the
right one, and nothing errored anywhere. I found it by testing it by hand: I
assigned a staff account to a different trip and checked whether the mobile view
and the trip permissions actually showed what they were supposed to. They did
not, and that is what exposed it. Nothing automated caught this — the build
passed and the test suite passed the whole time it was broken, because the
failure was silent. It is now one shared function with tests pinned on it, in
`frontend/src/lib/tripScope.js` and `tests/jq/tripScope.test.js`.

**Cancelled delegates.** This was not in the original design. I added it
afterwards, once I saw that staff and admins needed a clearer way to record
someone who was not coming. People dropping out at the last minute turned out to
be common, and without it there was no way to tell "not assigned to a coach yet"
apart from "not coming at all". It is a flag on the delegate rather than a new
status value.

**The "TEAMMATES DO NOT EDIT THIS FILE" rule.** I put that across my files so
that integration would not get messed up — four other people were building on
top of the same foundation, and if everyone edited it directly nothing would
have merged cleanly. I would still do it. But I do not think it should be
absolute. If a teammate's code genuinely needs to work together with mine, then
crossing that line is fine. The condition is that you check it does not destroy
any of the existing features first. Vimal ended up re-declaring some of my
database columns inside his own file rather than touching my schema, and that
worked, but it is the kind of thing that would have been simpler to just talk
about.

## Being honest about what I own

I would not claim I know every part of this codebase equally well. The parts
with my name on them, yes. But along the way some of the code came from
teammates because things had to work together, and the boundaries are not as
clean as the file headers make them look. I think that is normal on a project
this size, and it is more useful to say so than to pretend every line was a
deliberate decision I made on my own.
